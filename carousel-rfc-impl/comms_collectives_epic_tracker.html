02 First principles: why HBM round-trips are the enemy, and the ring physics

A Spyre accelerator is up to 32 cores, each with a small (~2 MiB) on-chip LX scratchpad, wired into a ring fabric. HBM is one flat off-chip space (~166 GB/s, no channel affinity). The matmul array is weight-stationary: activations stream past resident weights. When a producer computes an activation that already lives in LX, and the next consumer needs the same values under a different per-core ownership or layout, the compiler today spills the whole tensor to HBM and reloads it — a full write + read of bytes that never had to leave the chip. The epic replaces that round-trip with a direct on-chip LX→LX move.

The ring is not one flat "bandwidth number." Device measurement (additive-differential slope method, R² ≥ 0.998) shows the variable that sets effective bandwidth is the peak per-link transfer count — not burst size, not a hidden hardware cap. A uniform p→p+1 shift puts exactly one transfer on each link and reaches ~130 GB/s; an all-to-all scatter / linear fold piles many transfers onto the busiest link and collapses to a ~36 GB/s contention floor.

Uniform p→p+1 shift — one transfer per link, occ = 1
~130 GB/s (54@4MB · 90@8MB · 130@16MB) · MEASURED
All-to-all scatter / linear fold — 4–9 transfers on the busy link
~34–36 GB/s floor (524 descriptors) · MEASURED

Two hard consequences banked so far. (1) Burst is not the lever: enlarging per-descriptor burst 50–100× at fixed bytes moved effective bandwidth by 0.958× median (zero improvement) — the perf model assigns raw 128 to every link and only per-link contention pulls it down. (2) The bandwidth-optimal ring is unreachable for LX-resident operands: the ring-carousel-vs-naive crossover (~5.2 MiB/head) sits above the ~2 MiB/core LX cap, so any operand is force-tiled below the crossover and moves are F-dominated (~7.3 µs fixed per STCDP execute). The only real win banked to date is therefore HBM round-trip elimination, not ring transport efficiency — and every measured/modeled result below is tagged to keep that honest.

03 The convergence: two halves of one pipeline

Both halves meet at the DLDSC coordinate-metadata contract. Codex makes the move legal and value-correct; claude makes the planner select it and adds the arithmetic (reduce) collective codex explicitly parks. They are provably the same substrate at three points: codex's matmul_operand_broadcast == claude's attention QKᵀ all-gather (same class, same DCG region); codex's PR1 scatter is the complement of claude's Bet-2 cost term (mechanism + selectability of one edge); claude's reduce lane extends codex's parked reduce lane.
